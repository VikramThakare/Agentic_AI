# Neurological Deterioration Protocol

**Source**: Educational Prototype Guidelines
**Condition**: Sudden changes in vitals potentially linked to increased intracranial pressure (Cushing's triad: bradycardia, irregular respirations, widened pulse pressure) or sudden altered mental status.

## Assessment
- Perform an immediate rapid neurological exam (e.g., Glasgow Coma Scale, pupil reactivity).
- Check for signs of stroke (FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call).
- Evaluate recent vitals for sudden drops in Heart Rate coupled with high Blood Pressure.
- Check blood glucose levels to rule out severe hypoglycemia.

## Action Plan
- Elevate the head of the bed to 30 degrees to reduce intracranial pressure if safe.
- Maintain airway patency; prepare for intubation if GCS drops below 8.
- Notify the attending physician and neurology/stroke team immediately.
- Prepare the patient for an emergency CT scan of the head if stroke or hemorrhage is suspected.
- Do not administer hypotensive medications without specific orders, as cerebral perfusion pressure must be maintained.
